Toyota extended warranty in Australia, with every price shown

Integrity Car Care's Absolute Warranty for a used Toyota: Category 1 prices for every level and term, the 4WD surcharge, what to do about a hybrid, and what the claim caps mean against real Toyota repair bills.

Toyota's factory warranty is five years with unlimited kilometres on cars sold new since 2019, and most used Toyotas on the market are now past it. A Toyota is cheap to cover: nearly every model is Category 1, Integrity's lowest price band, so a 3-year Absolute Warranty on a Corolla or HiLux costs from $1,103. The Absolute Warranty, issued by Integrity Car Care, covers the parts Toyota's own warranty covered, except the PDS exclusions, with a cap per claim set by the level you choose. The one Toyota trap is hybrids, which are covered in their own section below. The prices here are the ones you pay; you can check your own car and buy online without giving us a phone number.

Toyota repair costs against the claim caps

Toyotas fail less often than most, which is why the warranty is cheap, but the repairs that do happen on a 6 to 12-year-old car are not small. The figures below are indicative ranges from Australian independent workshops for parts and labour; your own mechanic can give a figure for your model.

Worked example. A 2018 Corolla with 95,000 km is Category 1 and qualifies for every level. On Level 2 for 3 years the price is $1,478. If the CVT fails and Integrity approves a $5,200 replacement, Level 1 would pay $2,000 and leave you $3,200; Level 2 pays $5,000 and leaves you $200; Level 3 pays it in full for $304 more over the 3 years.

Second example. A 2017 HiLux SR5 4x4 with 130,000 km is Category 1, qualifies for Levels 1 to 3, and carries the $110 4WD surcharge. Level 2 for 2 years is $1,255 plus $110. A $3,800 injector job is paid in full; on Level 1 Integrity would pay $2,000.

Level 4 has no fixed cap: Integrity pays up to the market value of the car per claim. On a Category 1 car under 8 years and 120,000 km, Level 4 costs $214 to $268 a year more than Level 3. How to choose a level explains the trade-off in more detail.

Toyota hybrids: RAV4, Camry, Corolla, Kluger

This is where Toyota owners get caught. A hybrid is Category 3, not Category 1, and the Absolute Warranty excludes the parts that make it a hybrid: the high-voltage battery, the motor-generators and the inverter. Toyota's own hybrid battery warranty runs to 10 years when the car is inspected annually at a Toyota dealer; once that lapses, or if the service history breaks it, a replacement battery is a four-figure bill. Two products cover it: Total Cover Plus (hybrid, Category 3, from $2,960 for 1 year, battery up to $5,000 per 12 months) and the EV Warranty hybrid plans (from $1,094 for 12 months, battery limit $1,000 to $5,000 by plan). The hybrid and EV page compares them with every price. The quote tool asks for fuel type and only shows the products that fit.

Older or high-kilometre Toyotas

Toyotas get driven a long way, and Absolute stops at 15 years and 220,000 km on Level 1. Past that, the Endurance Warranty Option 1 has no age or kilometre limit and a fixed price for every make, $746 for 12 months. It covers 20 named components with a per-component limit ($2,000 for the engine, gearbox, differential and turbo on Option 1, $750 for the others), not the full manufacturer list, and the car must be serviced every 6 months or 10,000 km. For a 300,000 km Prado or a 2009 Camry it is the only option on the market, and it is priced the same as for any other car.

Still under Toyota's factory warranty

Two warranties can't run at the same time, so your cover starts the day after Toyota's warranty ends. The price and the levels you qualify for are set by the car's age and kilometres on the day you buy, not the day cover starts. Buying in the last months of the factory warranty usually means a lower price and a higher level than waiting. Toyota's capped-price servicing and Toyota Service Advantage don't conflict with a warranty; a dealer-sold extended warranty does, so tell us if you have one.

What isn't covered

Faults that existed before you bought the cover, wear and tear items (brakes, clutches, tyres, wipers, bulbs), missed or late servicing, modifications including lift kits and tunes, commercial use outside the PDS, and accident, flood or misuse. Bought privately: a condition report from a licensed mechanic is needed before the warranty is lodged, and it has to be clear of existing faults. A warranty is not car insurance. The full list is in the Absolute Warranty PDS; read it before you buy.
