Mercedes-Benz repairs out of factory warranty are priced for a premium car: an Airmatic strut, a transmission conductor plate or a turbo each sit in the thousands, and the parts come from Germany. The Absolute Warranty, issued by Integrity Car Care, covers the parts Mercedes-Benz's own warranty covered, except the PDS exclusions, with a cap per claim set by the level you choose. Mercedes models sit in Category 2, 3 or 4 depending on the model, so the first step is finding yours. The prices below are the ones you pay; you can check your own car and buy online without giving us a phone number.

Mercedes repair costs against the claim caps

Worked example. A 2019 Mercedes-Benz C 200 with 80,000 km is Category 2 and qualifies for every level. On Level 2 for 3 years the price is $2,326. If the transmission valve body fails and Integrity approves a $3,900 repair, Level 1 would pay $2,000 and leave you $1,900; Level 2 pays the full $3,900. The difference in price between Level 1 and Level 2 over 3 years is $375.

Second example. A 2017 GLE 350 d with 125,000 km is Category 2, qualifies for Levels 1 to 3, and carries the $110 4MATIC surcharge. Level 3 for 2 years is $2,433 plus $110. Two Airmatic struts at $5,800 are paid in full on Level 3; Level 2 would pay $5,000.

Level 4 has no fixed cap: Integrity pays up to the market value of the car per claim. On an 8-year-old Category 2 Mercedes under 120,000 km, the gap between Level 3 and Level 4 is $214 to $250 a year, which is small next to the difference between an $8,000 cap and the value of the car. How to choose a level explains the trade-off in more detail.

Older or high-kilometre models

Absolute accepts cars up to 15 years and 220,000 km at Level 1. Beyond that, the Endurance Warranty Option 1 has no age or kilometre limit and the same fixed price for every make, $746 for 12 months. The trade-off is that Endurance covers 20 named components with a per-component limit ($2,000 for the engine, gearbox, differential and turbo on Option 1, $750 for the others), not the full manufacturer list, and the car must be serviced every 6 months or 10,000 km. For a W204 C-Class or W164 ML past 200,000 km it is usually the only option, and the price is the same as for a Corolla.

Plug-in hybrids and EQ models

A C 300 e, E 300 e or GLC 300 e can be covered by Absolute (Category 3), but the high-voltage battery, electric motor and charging system are excluded. For those parts you need Total Cover Plus (hybrid, Category 3, from $2,960 for 1 year, battery up to $5,000 per 12 months) or the EV Warranty hybrid plans (from $1,094 for 12 months). A fully electric EQA, EQC or EQE is Category 4 and uses the EV Warranty electric plans, from $594 for 12 months with no age or kilometre limit on Plan A. The hybrid and EV page sets out every plan, limit and price.

Still under the factory warranty

Two warranties can't run at the same time, so your cover starts the day after the Mercedes-Benz warranty ends. The price and the levels you qualify for are set by the car's age and kilometres on the day you buy, not the day cover starts. Buying in the last months of the factory warranty usually means a lower price and a higher level than waiting until it has lapsed. If you have a dealer-extended Mercedes warranty or a service plan, tell us before you buy; service plans don't conflict, a second warranty does.

What isn't covered

Faults that existed before you bought the cover, wear and tear items (brakes, clutches, tyres, wipers, bulbs), missed or late servicing, modifications and tuning, track use, and accident, flood or misuse. Bought privately: a condition report from a licensed mechanic is needed before the warranty is lodged, and it has to be clear of existing faults. A warranty is not car insurance. The full list is in the Absolute Warranty PDS; read it before you buy.
