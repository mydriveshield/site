Used car warranty in Western Australia: the dealer statutory warranty, and what covers you after it

What a licensed WA dealer must repair, for how long, what is excluded, why a private sale gets nothing, and what an Integrity extended warranty costs for a car bought in Perth, Mandurah, Bunbury, Geraldton, Kalgoorlie and regional WA.

Buy a used car from a licensed dealer in Western Australia and the Motor Vehicle Dealers Act 1973 makes the dealer repair covered defects for a short period after sale. That period is the statutory warranty. It is short, it has exclusions, it does not exist on a private sale, and once it ends you are relying on the Australian Consumer Law or an extended warranty. The WA rule is below, with the government source, then what an extended warranty costs on top.

The WA rule

A licensed WA dealer must give a statutory warranty on a used passenger car priced at $4,000 or more. Up to 10 years old and up to 150,000 km: 3 months or 5,000 km, whichever comes first. Over 10 and up to 12 years, or 150,000 to 180,000 km: 1 month or 1,500 km. Over 12 years or over 180,000 km: none. The dealer must hand you an information statement describing which warranty applies. Source: WA Consumer Protection. The dealer repairs covered defects so the car is in reasonable condition for its age and kilometres; it is not a new-car warranty and it pays you no money.

Cars outside the rule

Cars under $4,000, over 12 years or over 180,000 km get no statutory warranty in WA. Single-row utes (tray or well body), trucks, caravans and buses for more than nine people are outside the scheme at any price; dual-cab utes and off-road 4WDs with two rows of seats are inside it. Only the Australian Consumer Law guarantees apply to the excluded vehicles. The national guide has every state's thresholds side by side.

What is excluded

Routine servicing and tune-ups, and defects caused by an accident or misuse. WA's scheme has a shorter exclusion list than the eastern states, but the 1-month tier for cars between 10 and 12 years is the shortest statutory period in the country.

Private sales in WA

None of this applies to a private sale. The statutory warranty is a dealer obligation, and the Australian Consumer Law guarantees cover businesses, not a person selling their own car. On a private sale in WA the protection is what you do before you pay: an independent pre-purchase inspection, a PPSR check, and the state's transfer paperwork. After the statutory warranty ends

For a dealer sale, the Australian Consumer Law guarantee of acceptable quality continues, judged against the car's age, price and kilometres, and enforced by you against the dealer, in a tribunal if it comes to that. For an older car that argument is weak. An extended warranty is a contract instead: a listed set of covered parts, a dollar cap per claim ($2,000, $5,000, $8,000 or market value on the Absolute Warranty), and a claims line that pays the repairer directly. WA is the only state with a one-month tier, so a 2015 car with 160,000 km bought in Perth has dealer cover for four weeks. That is the car an extended warranty is built for: at 11 years and 160,000 km it still qualifies for Absolute Level 1 and Level 2. Two warranties can't overlap, so cover bought during the statutory warranty starts the day after it ends.

A WA example

A 2015 Toyota HiLux SR 4x4 dual cab with 165,000 km bought from a Perth dealer for $32,000 gets the 1-month, 1,500 km statutory warranty. It is Category 1, qualifies for Absolute Levels 1 and 2, and carries the $110 4WD surcharge; Level 2 (cap $5,000) for 2 years is $1,255 plus $110. A single-cab tray HiLux of the same age gets no statutory warranty at all.
